# HTML Course

OBJECTIVE

Produce a complete course on the requested subject, delivered as a standalone HTML file that the reader opens in their browser, on a computer as well as on a phone. A course is not a book: it divides a body of knowledge into clear units, shows each one through examples, has the reader practice it, then sums it up so that it sticks. The reader learns it, checks that they grasp the whole, then reviews it, page after page. A single file can bring together several courses on neighboring subjects, which the reader works through one after the other or chooses from the contents; each course is built from the start so that it can take in others, as described in SEVERAL COURSES IN ONE FILE.

AUDIENCE

The course speaks at once to the novice, who is discovering the subject, and to the experienced reader, who comes to go deeper. It starts from what the novice already knows: every technical term receives its definition at the very place where it appears, and each lesson builds on the ones before it. The experienced reader finds their share in the precision of the distinctions and in the most demanding exercises.

COURSE ARCHITECTURE

Before writing, the AI maps out the subject and divides it into lessons, in the order in which it is best learned: the foundations first, then the heart of the subject, then its extensions. The number of lessons, parts, callout boxes and exercises follows what the subject calls for. Each lesson receives an accent color.

The course is made of pages that follow one another:

- the opening page: the banner, a short introduction that says what the course covers and whom it is for, how the lessons follow on from one another, how each lesson is built and how progress is kept, on its own in the browser when the browser allows it, and in the file through the Save button, which downloads the up-to-date course, to be opened next time in place of the old one; then the course outline and the course key;
- one page per lesson;
- the synthesis page: exercises that cut across several lessons;
- the review page: the review sheets, then the glossary.

Each page carries the data-cours attribute, which names the course it belongs to by a short letter specific to that course.

THE LESSON

Every lesson follows the same three-stage path.

Opening. The header carries the label “Lesson” in monospace and the number in a tag, then the title and a gradient separator. Next come two lists side by side, under a thin rule: the objectives, each preceded by an arrow → in the lesson's color, and the prerequisites, which name the lessons and notions this one builds on. Then the “To begin” block: a question or a concrete situation, large and in white, followed by a few sentences that show why the subject matters. The first part follows directly after this block; the contents give access to the parts of every lesson.

Body. The lesson moves forward in parts numbered in Roman numerals, from simple to complex, one idea at a time. The underlying pattern of each notion runs from the definition to the plain-language explanation, then to the example, the remarks and the check; the AI lets this pattern breathe according to the idea, tightening it, fleshing it out or changing the order when the idea is better understood another way, so that the text keeps the movement of a real explanation. Paragraphs are short and start flush with the margin. A table serves when the idea is compared or arranged in columns; a grid of cards serves when several elements of the same rank are presented together.

Closing. Four blocks close the lesson, each announced by a subheading:

- Key points: the lesson summed up in a few sentences, which are enough for review;
- Key terms: the terms of the lesson, as buttons; a tap shows the definition of the word, taken from the glossary, beneath the row;
- Exercises: exercises of increasing difficulty, leading from recalling the lesson to applying it, then to personal reflection; each carries a short name that says what it asks and its worked answer to unfold;
- Self-assessment: “I know…” statements to tick, one per objective, with a counter and a segmented bar.

THE SYNTHESIS

The synthesis checks that the reader holds the course as a whole. Each of its exercises draws on several lessons at once: a question that has the reader connect two notions, a situation that calls for combining several, a reflection that embraces the entire subject. They go from the simplest to the most demanding, and each carries its worked answer to unfold, which shows which lessons meet and how.

The page opens on a header labeled “Synthesis” in monospace, with a title and a gradient separator, then a brief note telling the reader to try the exercises before opening the answers. Each exercise uses the frame of the lesson exercises; beneath its level, a row of tags names the lessons it draws on, each in the color of its lesson and linking to it.

THE CALLOUT KEY

Each type of content always keeps the same form from one end of the course to the other, and the color follows the lesson. The key follows one principle: a frame marks a place where the reader acts, and what is read stands freely on the page background. Reading callouts are therefore set apart by a stroke, a rule or a band; “Remember” is the only reading callout that carries a frame, and it stands out all the more for it. Each callout carries a label, placed at the head of the callout, above its text. The opening page presents this key in a grid of cards with a 250px basis, gap 10px: each card is the callout itself, in its complete form, its label above the text, with a line that states its use.

- Definition: solid label in the lesson's color, black text, glyph ■; a 2px stroke in the color on the left edge, inset 2px 0 0, left padding of 20px; the defined term in bold in the color, followed by a colon and the definition. The label carries the single word “Definition”: the bold term that opens the text already names what the callout defines.
- Example: label outlined in the color, glyph ◆; a 1px dotted rule in the color at .6 on the left edge, left padding of 20px, text in white at .74: the example reads as an aside within the text.
- Caution: a 10px hatched band on the left edge, repeating-linear-gradient(135deg, rgba(255,255,255,.32) 0 2px, transparent 2px 6px), left padding of 26px; label outlined in white, glyph ▲.
- Remember: the double-ruled frame, inset 0 0 0 1px in the color at .8, then inset 0 0 0 5px in the page background, then inset 0 0 0 5.5px in the color at .35, padding 20px 26px 19px; white text at weight 500; label outlined in the color, glyph ●.
- Landmark: a point of reference that situates the notion, whether a date, a text, an author or a work, presented as a small timeline: a vertical 1px stroke in white at .18, left padding of 22px, and for each landmark a 7px square in the color set on the stroke and detached from it by a box-shadow ring 0 0 0 3px in the background color, the landmark in bold 11px monospace in the color, the text beneath; several landmarks follow one another on the same timeline, 14px apart; label outlined in white at .45, glyph ◇. The callout appears wherever the subject offers such points of reference.
- Check: it calls for an action and therefore keeps a frame, dashed, outline 1px dashed rgba(255,255,255,.3), transparent background, padding 16px 20px 15px; a question in white, then the “Show answer” button; label outlined in white at .5, glyph ○.

END OF THE COURSE

The review sheets gather, in a grid of cards with a 280px basis, gap 12px, the key points of each lesson: the script composes them from the “Key points” blocks, so that they always follow the text of the lessons. Each sheet stands without a frame, under a 2px top rule in the color of its lesson, padding 16px 2px 0, and carries the number and title of its lesson.

The glossary gathers all the key terms of the course in alphabetical order, each with a label that refers to the lesson where it is explained, in the color of that lesson. The glossary definitions are the ones the key-term buttons display. A discreet monospace footer closes the course.

VISUAL ATMOSPHERE

The course has a dark, dense atmosphere: dark background, absolute black, monospace tags, one vivid color per lesson. Its technique is that of a textbook: a reading column free of any frame, callouts that announce their role with a label at their head, frames reserved for what is done, and page-by-page reading.

Background and fonts. Plain #0a0a0a background on html and body. Frames, reserved for the elements where the reader acts (cards of the course outline, exercises, “Check” callouts, self-assessment statements) and for “Remember”, are in absolute black #000, with square corners, a hairline as box-shadow inset 0 0 0 .5px rgba(255,255,255,.2) and a line of light inset 0 1px 0 rgba(255,255,255,.04). The cut corner is reserved for small elements: tags, labels and buttons, with a 4px notch at the top right and bottom left, clip-path:polygon(0 0,calc(100% - 4px) 0,100% 4px,100% 100%,4px 100%,0 calc(100% - 4px)). Fonts. The text is set in four typefaces, each with its role. EB Garamond, stack "EB Garamond", Georgia, serif, carries all the reading text: it is the font of the page body, and the cards, tables, callouts, self-assessment, review sheets and glossary receive it with it. Cormorant Garamond, stack "Cormorant Garamond", "EB Garamond", Georgia, serif, at weight 600, carries the large titles: the banner title, the title of each lesson and the part titles, with their capitals and their letter-spacing. Inter, stack "Inter", "Helvetica Neue", Helvetica, Arial, "Segoe UI", system-ui, sans-serif, carries the framework of the page: the top bar, the contents and the page navigation. JetBrains Mono, stack "JetBrains Mono", SFMono-Regular, Menlo, Consolas, "Liberation Mono", monospace, carries everything the course writes in monospace: tags, labels, subheadings, captions, counts and buttons. The contrast between the text being read, in Garamond, and the machinery that carries it, in Inter and monospace, gives the course the look of a printed textbook served by an interface. The fonts are embedded in the file itself, so that the course displays in its own fonts everywhere, online or offline. The build script takes the woff2 files from the npm packages @fontsource/eb-garamond, @fontsource/cormorant-garamond, @fontsource/inter and @fontsource/jetbrains-mono, encodes them in base64 and declares each one with an @font-face rule, with its family, its style, its weight, font-display:swap and the unicode-range of its subset, taken from the package's index.css file. These rules form a stylesheet of their own, placed at the head of the page before the main stylesheet. EB Garamond enters it in the latin and latin-ext subsets, for accented transliterations, at weights 400, 500, 600 and 700, roman and italic; Cormorant Garamond in the latin subset, weight 600; Inter and JetBrains Mono in the latin subset, weights 400, 500, 600 and 700.

Body text in white at .82, calc(20.5px * var(--z,1.25)), line-height 1.5; the other reading texts follow the same scale, also written as calc(Npx * var(--z,1.25)): 18 to 19px for the lists of objectives and prerequisites, the key points, the self-assessment, the tables, the card titles and the glossary, 17px for card descriptions, review sheets and notes, so that all the text being read keeps a comfortable size at every step; bold and italic turn to full white. `*{box-sizing:border-box}` opens the stylesheet. Container #w centered, max-width 960px, padding 88px 24px 40px.

Lesson colors. Each lesson receives a vivid, saturated color, carried by a class that sets two variables: `--a` in hexadecimal and `--r` as space-separated RGB components, for transparencies written `rgb(var(--r)/.5)`. The colors follow one another in clearly distinct hues, from cool to warm. The color touches the lesson's tag and title, the separator, the Roman numerals, the labels, the bullets, the self-assessment segments, the active buttons, the progress bar and the top-bar caption. Subheadings, glows, frame hairlines and the save button stay white. The synthesis page, which brings all the lessons together, takes white as its color, `--a` #ffffff and `--r` 255 255 255; its header separator and its progress bar run through the colors of all the lessons, like the banner rule, so that the reader senses they are entering a page that gathers them together. In the top bar and in the page navigation, the caption that designates the synthesis is preceded by an 8px square with a cut corner, filled with the same gradient of the lesson colors.

Banner. Set directly on the page background, with no background of its own: its four L-shaped corners alone draw it. Padding 58px 28px 46px, centered, four 16x16px L-shaped corners at the exact angles, 1px strokes in rgba(255,255,255,.3), .6 on hover. Above the title, an overline in 11px monospace, uppercase, letter-spacing .28em, white at .55. Title in uppercase, bold, calc(32px * var(--z,1.25)), letter-spacing .18em with text-indent .18em. Subtitle in 12px monospace, uppercase, letter-spacing .08em, white at .75. Then a 1px rule that runs through the colors of all the lessons, from transparent to transparent, and the settings row: the text-size control and, to its right, the save button, described under TEXT SIZE and TOP BAR AND CONTENTS.

Subheadings. The name in bold 11px monospace, uppercase, letter-spacing .14em, full white, followed on the same line by a gradient hairline rgba(255,255,255,.3) → .15 at 33% → .05 at 66% → transparent; margin-top 60px. A note of one or two sentences may follow, in calc(17px * var(--z,1.25)), white at .55.

Overall progress. Between the subheading of the course outline and its grid, a line gives the progress of the whole course: a continuous 4px bar on a white background at .08, divided into segments proportional to the number of statements in each lesson and separated by 2px, each segment filling in its lesson's color to the extent of what has been mastered; on the right, the count “n / N mastered” in bold 11px monospace, white. The same count stands at the head of the contents, beneath the settings row.

Card grids. The course outline, the course key, the card grids of the body and the review sheets are laid out in display:flex with flex-wrap:wrap: each card receives flex:1 1 its basis width and min-width:0. The cards of a row share its whole width in equal parts, and those of the last row widen in the same way to fill it: the grid always forms a full block, edge to edge.

Course outline. A grid of cards with a 260px basis, gap 12px, one card per lesson: numbered tag aligned left, title in uppercase calc(16px * var(--z,1.25)) letter-spacing .06em, one-line description in calc(15px * var(--z,1.25)), white at .55, then the self-assessment progress, a 6px segmented bar and the count “n / N mastered” in 9.5px monospace. Each card leads to its lesson. A final card, at flex-basis 100%, leads to the synthesis and the review. When all the statements of a lesson are ticked, the count on its card gives way to the label “✓ Mastered”, in bold 10px monospace, uppercase, letter-spacing .14em, padding 3px 8px, cut corner, background in the lesson's color, black text. On hover, a glow filter:drop-shadow(0 0 16px rgba(255,255,255,.09)) and hairline at .35.

Lesson header. Numbered tag in bold 12px monospace, padding 3px 8px, outlined in the color. Title in uppercase, bold, calc(30px * var(--z,1.25)), letter-spacing .1em, in the color. 1px separator: color at .7 → .35 at 25% → .12 at 50% → .04 at 75% → transparent.

Opening. Grid 1.45fr 1fr, gap 0 40px, margin-top 26px, padding-top 18px under a top rule inset 0 .5px 0 rgba(255,255,255,.18); both lists without background or frame; each objective takes a left indent of 1.5em, which follows the text size, and its arrow sits on the left within that indent, so that a clear space always separates the arrow from the first word; captions in 10px monospace, uppercase, letter-spacing .16em, white at .5. The “To begin” block, margin-top 28px, takes a background in the color at .05 and a left bar inset 2px 0 0 in the color; the question in calc(23px * var(--z,1.25)), white, weight 600.

Parts. The part title in uppercase, bold, calc(21px * var(--z,1.25)), letter-spacing .08em, white, preceded by the Roman numeral in a tag outlined in the color at .7; a short neutral gradient hairline beneath it; margin-top 60px.

Reading column and labels. The content of the parts and of the closing stands in a reading column of 35em, which grows with the text and keeps a line of about seventy characters at every step. Callouts sit in it with margin 26px 0, without background. Each callout opens with its label, placed above its text, margin-bottom 10px: bold 10px monospace, uppercase, letter-spacing .14em, padding 3px 8px, cut corner, glyph at 9px before the name. Label and text thus form a single block, read at a glance, from top to bottom.

Justified text. The reading text is justified on both edges and its words are hyphenated at the end of lines: text-align:justify and hyphens:auto, doubled with -webkit-hyphens:auto, always go together, and the page's lang attribute, which carries the language of its content (lang="en" for English), gives the browser the hyphenation dictionary of that language. Justification gives the reading column a clean right edge and spreads between the words the air that would otherwise remain at the end of the line; hyphenation keeps that air in check, because it lets a line take the first half of a long word instead of stopping before it. Both rules apply to continuous paragraphs: the introduction of the opening page, the sentences of the “To begin” block that follow the question, the paragraphs of the parts, the text of the Definition, Example, Caution and Remember callouts, and the unfolded answers and worked answers. Short texts are set ragged right with hyphens:none, and keep a free right edge and whole words: the model callouts of the course key on the opening page, the “To begin” question, the lists of objectives and prerequisites, the Key points bullets, the cards, the tables, the labels and buttons, the questions of the exercises and of the “Check” callouts, the self-assessment statements, the review sheets, the glossary, the contents and the page navigation. Titles follow the rule of their own, described under PHONE. The spacing between letters and between words of the justified text remains that of the font, letter-spacing:normal and word-spacing:normal: justification alone lets the line breathe.

Tables and grids. Table without background, rows separated by .5px rules at .12 and a .5px bottom rule at .18, headers in bold 10px monospace in the color underlined by a rule in the color at .5, first column in bold white. Grid of cards with a 250px basis, gap 10px: each reading card stands without a frame, under a 1px top rule in the color at .55, padding 14px 2px 0, with its tag, its white title and its description at .66.

Closing. The key points stand without a frame, under a 2px top rule in the color, padding 18px 0 0, white text, 6px square bullets in the color. Key terms as 10.5px monospace buttons, uppercase, hairline .3, cut corner; the active word takes the color as background, bold black text. Exercise in a frame: at its head, the exercise number in the color, three 14x5px bars whose filled ones state the difficulty on a scale of three, and the exercise's name; the question in white. Self-assessment: each statement in a clickable frame, an 18px box with a cut corner that fills with the color and carries a black check mark, drawn in SVG with a 2px stroke, the text turning white when the box is ticked.

Synthesis. The tags of the lessons drawn on, in 9.5px monospace, padding 2px 7px, cut corner, outlined in the color of their lesson, line up beneath the exercise's name with a gap of 6px.

PAGES AND NAVIGATION

The course is read one page at a time. On load, the script gathers the opening content into a first page, then treats each lesson, the synthesis and the review as so many pages; the current page carries the class that displays it.

At the bottom of each page, a navigation bar: a 1px white gradient rule, then two links without frames, “← Previous page” on the left and “Next page →” on the right, padding 10px 4px, each with its caption in bold 10px monospace in the color of the target page and, beneath it, the title of that page in bold white, which lights up on hover with a text-shadow 0 0 14px rgba(255,255,255,.35); in the center, the page number “02 / 08” in 11px monospace, counted within the course the page belongs to. On the first page of the file, the previous link fades away and keeps its place; on the last, the next link does the same. When the file brings several courses together, navigation passes from one course to the next without stopping: the review page of one course leads to the opening of the next course, and the title of a page that belongs to another course is preceded by the name of that course, “Course name · Opening”. The left and right arrow keys of the keyboard also turn the pages.

Each page change displays the target page at its top, or at the target location when a link leads there, updates the address through history.replaceState, the top-bar caption and the color of the progress bar. Every internal link, from the contents, the course outline, the synthesis or the glossary, opens the page that contains its target and scrolls down to it. When the file opens, an address that designates a place in the course leads there; failing that, the course reopens the remembered page, and the reading position is resumed as described in SAVED PROGRESS.

TOP BAR AND CONTENTS

Fixed top bar of 52px, background rgba(10,10,10,.9) with backdrop-filter blur(12px) doubled with -webkit-backdrop-filter, bottom hairline. On the left, the name of the course of the displayed page, in 11px monospace, uppercase, letter-spacing .2em, which changes when the reader moves from one course to another; on the right, the current page (“Lesson 03 · title”, “Opening”, “Synthesis”, “Review”) in the color of the page, then the Contents button. Beneath it, a 2px progress bar in the color of the page, with a glow, which advances according to the page and the scrolling within the page, measured over the pages of the displayed course.

The contents are a drawer that slides in from the right, as described in ANIMATIONS, width min(360px,90vw), over a veil rgba(0,0,0,.6). At its head it carries the settings row, the text-size control on the left and the save button to its right, which extends to the right edge of the drawer, then the count of what has been mastered across the whole file. When the file brings several courses together, the course tabs come next, described in SEVERAL COURSES IN ONE FILE, and the list that follows is that of the chosen course. This list gives the opening, each lesson with its tag, its title and its mastered count, replaced by a ✓ in the lesson's color when the lesson is mastered, its parts beneath it preceded by their Roman numeral, then the synthesis, and finally the review sheets and the glossary. The entry of the displayed page receives the highlight rgba(20,20,20,.92) and the bar inset 2px 0 0 #fff. The script builds the contents from the pages; a tap on an entry closes the drawer, and so does the Escape key.

Save button. The “Save” button stands to the right of the text-size control, on the same row, in the banner of the opening page and at the head of the contents. The row is in display:flex, align-items:stretch, gap 12px; centered in the banner, aligned left in the contents. The button thus takes the height of the A−, A, A+ buttons: bold 10px monospace, uppercase, letter-spacing .14em, padding 0 14px, white at .75, hairline inset 0 0 0 .5px rgba(255,255,255,.3), cut corner, display inline-flex, align-items center, justify-content center. Before the caption, a round 6px dot, margin-right 8px, which says whether the file carries the present progress: white at .9 with the glow box-shadow 0 0 6px rgba(255,255,255,.6) when a box has changed since the progress was last written into the file, white at .2 when the file already carries it; it passes from one state to the other in .3s. The same dot, lit, sits on the Contents button of the top bar, at the top right, 4px from its edges, so that the reader sees from every page that progress is waiting to be saved. On click, the course downloads the up-to-date file, as described in SAVED PROGRESS, and the button shows “Saved” on a white background, black text, then returns to its caption after 1.6s. The button and the dot appear when the course is opened directly, which the comparison parent===window establishes; set in a frame, the course entrusts its file to the host page, which keeps it up to date itself, and the row keeps only the text-size control.

TEXT SIZE

A factor `--z` set on the root element governs all reading text, written calc(Npx * var(--z,1.25)). Six steps lead from a tight text to an ample one: 0.85, 0.925, 1, 1.08, 1.16, 1.25. The course opens at the most ample step, 1.25, which is its default size: the reader immediately finds the most comfortable text and tightens it as they wish. This step is also the fallback value of every `var(--z,1.25)`, so that the course appears at this size from the very first frame. The A− button goes down one step, the A+ button goes up one step, the A button returns the course to its default size. The three buttons A−, A, A+, at 11, 13 and 15px, min-width 38px, padding 7px 9px, hairline .2, cut corner, stand in the banner and at the head of the contents, followed on their right by the save button. The button that reaches the end of the range fades, to .3 opacity with pointer-events:none: at opening, A+ thus stands back and tells the reader that the text is at its largest. Controls, monospace captions and geometry keep their measurements.

UNFOLDING

Answers and worked answers unfold beneath their button through a one-row grid: grid-template-rows 0fr → 1fr in .35s cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.3,1), return in .25s cubic-bezier(.4,0,1,1); the child carries min-height:0 and overflow:hidden; the content enters with opacity 0 → 1 and translateY(6px) → 0, preceded by a white gradient separator with 14px of air. The button, in 10px monospace, uppercase, hairline .32, cut corner, changes from “Show answer” to “Hide answer” and then takes the color as background, bold black text. The definition of a key term unfolds in the same way; another word replaces it with a crossfade, and the same word folds it back up.

HOVER AND TOUCH

All hover rules live in `@media (hover:hover) and (pointer:fine)`. On hover, the save button takes a hairline at .6 and white text. The touch regime, `@media not ((hover:hover) and (pointer:fine))`, gives cards, clickable frames and buttons a brief :active feedback, background rgba(255,255,255,.04). An empty touchstart listener set on the document makes this feedback visible on iPhone. Controls receive -webkit-tap-highlight-color:transparent and touch-action:manipulation.

ANIMATIONS

Principle. Every movement accompanies a gesture of the reader and answers it: an entrance reads as an unfolding, an exit as a packing away; entrances are slower than exits, and every movement ends in less than half a second. Entrances follow cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.3,1), exits cubic-bezier(.4,0,1,1). One-off animations go through the Web Animations interface, element.animate(), and are cancelled with cancel() when they end, which returns each element to its base style.

Page change. The page being left exits with opacity 1 → 0 over 150ms. The target page then enters with opacity 0 → 1 and translateY(10px) → 0 over 380ms, then, in a cascade, the first three blocks of the page join it with 50ms between two starts, on the same movement. Scrolling settles at the top of the target page, or at the target location, between the exit and the entrance. A new page change during a movement cancels the movement in progress and starts the new one.

Contents drawer. The drawer slides from translateX(100%) to 0 in .38s on opening and returns in .26s on closing, while the veil goes from opacity 0 to 1 and back over the same durations. On opening, the contents entries enter with opacity 0 → 1 and translateX(12px) → 0, 25ms between two starts, the last start falling at 300ms at the latest. The entry of the displayed page takes its highlight and its bar in .25s.

Ticking a statement. The box fills with the color in .2s, and the black check mark draws itself from left to right, through a stroke-dashoffset that goes from its length to 0 in .28s. At the same moment, the box grows to 1.18 at a third of the animation then returns to 1, while a ring in the color at .6 widens from 0 to 10px around it as it fades, over 520ms. The text turns white in .25s. Unticking erases the check mark and the background in .18s, without a ring.

Lesson mastered. When the last statement of a lesson is ticked, the segmented bar of the self-assessment lights up with a glow in the color, filter drop-shadow(0 0 10px) at .6, which rises then fades over 900ms; in the course outline and the contents, the label “✓ Mastered” and the ✓ enter with opacity 0 → 1 and scale(.85) → 1 over 320ms, the next time they appear on screen.

Progress. Each self-assessment segment fills in .35s, in order, 30ms between two segments. The segments of the overall progress and the progress bar of the top bar glide in .6s cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.2,1); on page change, the top bar changes color over the same duration. The “n / N” counters change digits with a crossfade, 120ms out then 180ms in.

Hover and touch. Hairlines, glows, backgrounds and colors of cards, clickable frames and buttons change in .25s on hover; the :active feedback of the touch regime sets in within .08s and fades in .2s. The key-term and answer buttons take on and leave the color as background in .2s.

Reduced motion. Under prefers-reduced-motion, read through matchMedia, durations drop to .01ms, delays to 0, and the ring, the lesson-mastered glow and the cascades stay at rest.

SAVED PROGRESS

Three keepers. The course entrusts its progress to three keepers and serves them at every gesture: the file itself, which carries the written progress, kept up to date by the host page or by the save button; the browser's local storage, when the course is opened directly, as a file or as a page, under the key “html-course:” followed by the subject of the course; the host page, when the course is set in an isolated frame, where local storage belongs to the page and where the frame addresses it by message. Writes are made inside a try, and the course goes on its way with whatever it obtains.

Written progress. The file carries in its head, just before the closing of head, a block reserved for progress: `<script type="application/json" id="lv-progression">`, which contains a JSON object {"maj": time of the last change in milliseconds, "acquis": {statement key: true}}. A ticked statement appears in it with the value true; an unticked statement leaves it. A new course carries the block with maj at 0 and acquis empty. This block is the part of the file that the host page and the save button rewrite: the file the reader gets back thus carries all of their progress.

Keys. Each “I know…” statement carries a stable key, written once in the source file of its lesson, made of letters from a to z, capitals included, digits, dots, hyphens or underscores, 80 characters at most: thus l03.2 for the second statement of lesson 3 of the first course; the following courses replace the letter l with their course's letter, thus g03.2, so that the keys of all the courses in the file coexist in the same progress block. The key stays attached to its statement when the course is reorganized, and it is under this key that the host page and the save button write the tick into the file.

State. The state brings together the mastered statements; the time of the last change of a box, under the name maj; the page reached; the reading position within that page, as a proportion of its height, from 0 to 1, which keeps it accurate at every size step; and finally the size step. It forms the object {v:1, maj, acquis, page, pos, taille}.

Sending. The state is recomposed from the document and sent to local storage and to the host page at every gesture: box ticked or unticked, page change, step change. The reading position is sent during scrolling, at a pace set by requestAnimationFrame. The state is also sent when the page is hidden or left, on visibilitychange to the hidden state and on pagehide. Toward the host page, the sending is written parent.postMessage({lvEtat:etat},'*'), and it takes place when the course is actually set in a frame, which the comparison parent!==window establishes. At every box ticked or unticked, the course also sends, under the same condition, parent.postMessage({lvProgression:{maj:maj,acquis:acquis}},'*'), in the exact form of the block: the host page writes it into the file. Opened directly, the course adjusts, at every box ticked or unticked, the dots of the save button and of the Contents button: they light up when maj differs from that of the progress written in the copy of the file.

Resumption. At the first instant of the script, the course reads the written block and local storage: the mastered statements come from the more recent of the two according to maj, the rest of the state comes from local storage, and the course sets itself up from it. It then asks the host page for its state with parent.postMessage({lvEtatVoulu:1},'*'), under the same condition. The page replies with a message {lvEtat:etat}, and it may repeat it several times during setup. The course welcomes the messages whose source is its parent and applies the received state on each arrival, until the reader's first gesture, which opens their own reading. The mastered statements again come from the more recent according to maj, between the received state and the written progress; the rest comes from the received state. The received state is applied all at once: a class on the root element suspends all transitions, the remembered page appears, the boxes in their state, the counters, the segmented bars, the overall progress and the “Mastered” labels at their level; the class is removed two frames later. Opened on its own, the course scrolls back down to the remembered reading position once the size is applied; set in a frame, it receives its reading place from the host page, which keeps it and restores it itself. When the progress thus resumed differs from that of the block, the course sends lvProgression once, and the file comes into line with it; opened directly, it lights up the save dots.

Up-to-date file. The script stands as the last element of body. Its first act, before gathering the pages or touching the document, is to keep a copy of the course's source: the declaration <!DOCTYPE html>, a line break, then document.documentElement.outerHTML, which at this instant carries the whole document as it was delivered, fonts included. The save button composes the up-to-date file from this copy: it replaces the content of the progress block with the present progress, {maj, acquis}, written by JSON.stringify, where every opening angle bracket takes its JSON escaped form, a backslash followed by u003c, so that the block remains a plain data block. It makes it into a Blob of type text/html;charset=utf-8 and downloads it through a temporary link carrying the download attribute, added to the document, clicked then removed; the object's address is released one second later through URL.revokeObjectURL. The downloaded file takes the name of the opened file, taken from the last part of location.pathname, decoded, when it ends in .html, and failing that the name taken from the course title, or from the common subject when the file brings several courses together. The kept copy in turn receives the written progress, and the save dots go out. The reader then opens this file in place of the old one; on a phone, the browser stores it in its downloads or in the Files app. When it opens, resumption reads the block and finds all the ticked boxes again, in any browser.

SEVERAL COURSES IN ONE FILE

A course file can take in others. When the reader hands over an existing file and asks for a new course “in it”, the AI adds this course to the file, after the courses already present, and delivers the whole file. Each added course is a complete course, with its own opening page, its lessons, its synthesis and its review page, composed according to all the rules of this skill.

Identity of each course. Each course receives a short letter, carried by the data-cours attribute of all its pages: the first course keeps the letter l, each added course takes a new letter drawn from its subject. The identifiers of its lessons are written with this letter followed by the number, its parts likewise, and its opening, synthesis and review pages as well as the anchors of its review sheets, its glossary and its outline carry the same letter as a suffix. Lessons are numbered from 1 in each course and take up the lesson colors again from the first: each course forms its own arc from cool to warm.

What is shared. The file keeps a single progress block, a single local storage key, that of the first course, a single text-size control, a single reading state and a single copy of its source, which each save button downloads in full, so that the reader's progress in the courses already present is kept in its entirety. The page title, in the title tag, and the file name take the common subject of the courses brought together. The stylesheet, the fonts and the script are common to all the courses: the script recognizes the courses from the data-cours attribute and builds for each its outline, its review sheets, its counts and its navigation.

What remains specific to each course. Each course has its glossary, in a data table of its own, `<script type="application/json" id="lv-glossaire-x">` where x is the letter of the course, the first course keeping `lv-glossaire`; the key-term buttons of a lesson read the glossary of its course. Each course has its overall progress, its synthesis page whose lesson tags lead to its own lessons, its review sheets and its sorted glossary. The banner of each opening carries as its overline the place of the course in the file and the number of its lessons, “Course 2 of 2 · eight lessons”, and the overline of the courses already present is updated at each addition. Each opening banner carries the settings row, text size and save button.

The outline links the courses. In the outline of each course, after the final card that leads to the synthesis and the review, a card at flex-basis 100% leads to the opening of each of the other courses: a caption in 9.5px monospace, uppercase, letter-spacing .16em, white at .5, “Course 1 · the other course”, then the course title in uppercase and its subtitle, with the arrow → on the right.

Contents tabs. Beneath the progress line, a grid in repeat(auto-fit,minmax(140px,1fr)), gap 8px, carries one tab per course, in the manner of a choice between cards: each tab is a button on a #000 background, hairline .2, cut corner, padding 11px 12px 10px, which stacks the caption “Course 1” in bold 9.5px monospace, uppercase, letter-spacing .16em, white at .5, the course title in bold 10.5px monospace, uppercase, letter-spacing .08em, white at .7, and the “n / N” count of what has been mastered in the course. The chosen tab takes the background rgba(255,255,255,.06), a hairline at .55 and a 2px top stroke in white, and its caption and title turn white. A tap on a tab displays the list of that course, whose entries enter in a cascade as when the drawer opens. At each opening, the contents settle on the tab of the course of the displayed page. The tabs follow the common hover and touch regime, and measure at least 44px high on a phone, where their texts go to 11px. A file with a single course presents its list directly.

The courses answer one another. Each course can be followed on its own: it recalls in a sentence, at their first use, the basic terms that another course in the file defines, and it keeps its Definition callouts for the terms it brings. Its prerequisites and its text can refer to the lessons of another course through a link to their identifier, naming the course, “lesson 3 of the course on such-and-such subject”. The opening of an added course carries what is specific to it: its banner, what it covers, the sequence of its lessons and its outline. It says in one sentence how it answers the other courses in the file, and, in the same sentence or the next, that its lessons share the construction, the callout key and the saved progress presented in the opening of the first course, with a link to it. These common presentations thus stand only once in the file, in the opening of the first course.

UPDATING A COURSE

When the reader hands over an existing course to have it evolve, the AI takes up its progress block as it is and keeps the key of every statement that is kept, even when reworded or moved; a new statement receives a new key. It reads the block to learn the reader's progress and makes the course evolve accordingly: what they have mastered keeps its place, the lessons still little ticked can be clarified and enriched with examples, and the synthesis builds on what they already know. When the reader hands over an existing file to add a course to it, the AI takes up the file as it is, present courses, progress block and storage key included, then adds the new course to it and adapts the script, the contents and the overlines according to SEVERAL COURSES IN ONE FILE. A course composed before the save button receives it at every update, to the right of the text-size control, with its dot on the Contents button, the sentence that presents it in the introduction and the script placed as the last element of body.

PHONE

A single breakpoint, `@media (max-width:639.98px)`. The top bar carries the current page on the left and the Contents button on the right, the course name giving up its place; the page caption fits on one line, with white-space:nowrap, overflow:hidden and text-overflow:ellipsis, in an element with flex:1 and min-width:0; #w goes to padding 76px 16px 32px; banner corners at 12px; title at calc(22px * var(--z,1.25)), letter-spacing .12em with text-indent .12em; lesson title at calc(23px * var(--z,1.25)). The settings row keeps the text-size control and the save button on a single line, each button 44px high. The opening goes to one column; “Check” at padding 14px 16px, “Remember” at padding 18px 20px 17px. Tables stack: each row becomes a block, each cell preceded by the name of its column in 9.5px monospace in the color, which the script takes from the headers. Page navigation goes to one column: the page number, then the next page, then the previous page; the faded link then leaves all the room to the others. Small monospace texts (callout labels, tags, frame captions, mastered counts, column names, save button) take 11px at the minimum, to remain readable on a small screen. Everything that is touched measures at least 44px high.

Titles, from the banner to the parts, carry overflow-wrap:anywhere and hyphens:auto, so that a long word breaks at the edge of the screen.

The page extends to the edges of notched screens: the viewport tag carries viewport-fit=cover, and the margins follow the safe areas of the screen. The top bar receives padding-top env(safe-area-inset-top) and grows by as much; #w adds env(safe-area-inset-left) and env(safe-area-inset-right) to its side margins and env(safe-area-inset-bottom) to its bottom margin; the contents drawer adds the top, right and bottom safe areas to its padding.

TYPOGRAPHY

The text follows the typographic conventions of the language of its content. In English: curly apostrophe ’, curly double quotes “ ”, with single quotes ‘ ’ for a quotation inside a quotation, and no space before : ; ! ?. Commas, parentheses and colons carry the asides. In French: curly apostrophe ’, French guillemets « » with a narrow non-breaking space inside, a narrow non-breaking space before ; ! ?, a non-breaking space before the colon; within a quotation, reported speech takes English quotes “ ”. A pass of the build script applies these rules to the text located outside tags, styles and scripts.

WRITING WITH SEVERAL HANDS

The lessons can be entrusted to subagents who write in parallel, each one or several lessons in their own source file. The main AI then keeps charge of the unity of the course, from the opening page to the glossary.

A shared brief precedes the writing. The main AI writes it in a file that each subagent reads in full before writing. In it, it sets out the map of the subject and the complete outline of the course, the terms already defined by the other courses in the file with the lesson that defines them, each one's share, the objectives and prerequisites of each lesson, the audience, the tone, the callout key, the rules of substance and form, the markup, the form of the statement keys and the form in which each lesson is delivered.

Basic terms are defined once, in the lesson where the reader meets them for the first time; the following lessons use them directly and name that lesson in their prerequisites.

A single-handed rereading follows the writing. Before assembly, the main AI reads the whole course itself, lesson after lesson, in the reader's order. It gives the whole text a single voice. It attunes each lesson to those that precede it, so that each builds on what they have laid down. It gives each example and each quotation one main place in the whole file, the one where they serve best, and brings them back elsewhere when the new place draws from them an insight of its own that fully justifies their return. It rewrites the passages and exercises that repeat what another lesson already carries, replacing them with new material. It gathers the key terms of all the lessons into a single glossary, where each word keeps the definition of the lesson that explains it. It then composes the synthesis itself, on the reread course, since the synthesis cuts across all the lessons. The course then reads as the work of a single author, and each lesson keeps its full substance.

METHOD AND DELIVERY

Each lesson is written in its own source file, the synthesis in its own, with the header, the progress block and the stylesheet in one file, the end of the course and the script in another, and the glossary in a data table. A Python script assembles the files in order, composes the glossary sorted alphabetically, applies the typography and writes the course. The course's script is the last element of body.

The course is delivered as a single HTML file, CSS and JavaScript inline, fonts included, with a lang attribute that carries the language of its content (lang="en" for English), the viewport tag width=device-width, initial-scale=1, viewport-fit=cover, and -webkit-text-size-adjust:100% on html. The file name takes up the title of the course, or the common subject when the file brings several courses together. At print time, all the pages are displayed one after the other, each lesson and the synthesis on a new sheet, worked answers unfolded, and the save button is removed from the printed page.
