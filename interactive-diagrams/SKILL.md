OBJECTIVE AND CONTENT

Produce an interactive HTML learning widget, delivered as a standalone file and published as a page of its own, where the reader consults it alone. The widget gives a structured overview from which the reader explores in depth: every element contains its explanation, written at build time, which unfolds on click. It is rich and dense.

Audience. The diagram speaks at once to the novice, who does not even know the subject exists, and to the experienced reader, who comes to deepen what they already know. Everything written in the widget therefore starts from what the novice already knows: the subject is named plainly, every technical term receives its definition at the very spot where it appears, in a few words within the sentence that introduces it, and the order of the sections leads the reader from the known to the new. The experienced reader finds their share in the same text: the density, the precision of the distinctions and the explanations that open up the depths of the subject. A single piece of writing serves both, the brief definition in passing letting the development carry on at its full level. The scopes and the depths serve the same double audience: the core scope and the abridged depth give the novice a clear door onto the heart of the subject, the complete scope and the full depth give the experienced reader the full extent and the full detail of the ground.

VISUAL PRINCIPLES

General aesthetic. A professional tool. A dark, elegant, restrained interface, where information density takes precedence over decoration. Generous spacing. Thin, discreet borders, 1px or a 0.5px hairline. Typography alone carries the decoration: hierarchy is read through size and weight. The visual vocabulary is made of cut corners in clip-path polygon on the cards and the numbered tag, numbered monospace tags (01, 02, 03…), monospace for secondary labels, gradient separators, and left side bars.

Backgrounds. The solid #0a0a0a background is set on html and on body, and so covers the whole page, on a computer as in a phone's browser: below the widget's last element, with sections collapsed, it is this same background that continues. The card is the only element to receive absolute black #000000. All the other structural elements (section header, subgroup header, branch header, fork container, tension row, timeline, section content, section container) let the #0a0a0a show through at rest. Section, subgroup and branch headers and the banner take a background on hover, in the form of a dark gray band. The header of an open section, then in sticky position, receives a permanent background rgba(10,10,10,0.92) with backdrop-filter:blur(8px), always doubled with -webkit-backdrop-filter:blur(8px), which veils the content scrolling behind it. The banner rests freely on the background, borderless. The main container is limited to the height of its content.

Typography. A sans-serif interface font, the system stack: -apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, "Segoe UI", Roboto, sans-serif. Monospace for technical elements and tags. Letter-spacing differs by level and creates a readable hierarchy even before sizes are compared: 0.18em on the banner's main title, 0.12em on section titles, 0.08em on subtitles and the banner subtitle, 0.05em on monospace tags, 0.04em on card titles. Letter-spacing applied to centered text leaves a residual space after the last letter: on the banner title and subtitle, a text-indent equal to the letter-spacing value (for example text-indent:0.18em on the title) restores optical centering.

Color. For a simple, unified subject, the widget stays entirely neutral, in white and dimmed white. For a subject structured in large distinct phases, the sections are grouped into thematic blocks and each block receives a vivid, saturated color, the blocks following one another in clearly distinct hues: color is assigned per block. Color touches section titles (text and tag), gradient separators, the disc that precedes subgroup headers and the titles of fork branches. Everything else remains white or dimmed white: the hover halos, white in every circumstance, the name and the rule of the block heading, white in every widget, the inner borders of the cards (white at 0.2 alpha at rest, 0.35 on hover), and the structural elements of the special layouts (Y fork, central line, tension connector, timeline dots).

Each color block is named by a heading, described under “Block heading”. The heading turns a change of hue into information: it says on what grounds its sections belong together, and it is read before anything is opened. Headings therefore accompany color, and a neutral widget does without them as it does without color.

Legibility and hover. At rest, elements read clearly; on hover, they light up. Section titles, card titles, subgroup titles and main values are in full white #ffffff at rest. The monospace descriptions of subgroups have a color alpha of at least 0.8 at rest, rising to 1 on hover. Secondary texts and card descriptions have an alpha of 0.55 at rest, rising to 0.85 on hover. On hover, titles receive a subtle text-shadow, always white, on neutral sections as on colored sections. The color transition applies to each text child individually. All of the widget's hover rules live in the fine-pointer media query described under MOBILE ADAPTATION.

Hover follows two logics according to the nature of the element. Section, subgroup and branch headers, the block heading and the banner take a simple change of background, which produces a band visible by contrast with the black: rgba(20,20,20,.92) for headers and the heading, rgba(255,255,255,.015) for the banner. Cards take a concentrated halo and a more visible inner border, inset 0 0 0 .5px at 0.35 alpha.

Card halo. The card is made of two levels: the wrapper, the clickable element that carries the halo, and the body, its only child, which carries the cut-corner clip-path, the #000 background, the padding and the box-shadows. The halo goes through filter:drop-shadow(0 0 16px rgba(255,255,255,.09)) set on the wrapper: it applies to the already clipped body and follows the cut shape. This drop-shadow is white everywhere, like the inset border: colored sections keep it white just as neutral sections do, the block's color living in the texts, the tags, the separators and the discs. The halo receives its own transition, filter .25s ease, in step with the texts that accompany it. On hover, the card stays exactly in place: the halo and the texts alone carry the change.

The widget's movement is limited to seven things: hover transitions, the opening cascade, the collapsing cascade, the unfolding of explanations, the rearrangement of the cards of a parallel grid, the fade-in of lines drawn in JavaScript, and the rotation of the chevrons, the unfold signs and the global arrow. Everything else in the widget is still.

Unfolding explanations. The widget carries all its knowledge within itself. Every element that names a subject (banner, block heading, section title, subgroup header, card, branch header, tension column label, tension connector, timeline card) contains its explanation, written according to the EXPLANATIONS part. The explanation is folded at rest; a click on the element unfolds it, a second click folds it again. Each element unfolds independently of the others, and the reader opens as many explanations as they wish. On load, every explanation waits folded.

Place of the explanation. In a card, the explanation unfolds inside the card itself, below the description, preceded by a 1px separator in a neutral gradient (rgba(255,255,255,0.3) → 0.15 → 0.05 → transparent) with 12px of air on either side: the card grows and stays a single piece, cut corner included. For an element without a frame of its own (banner, heading, section title, subgroup header, branch header, column label), the explanation unfolds just below the element, in a panel that extends its header: same background, text aligned on the first letter of the title, bottom padding of 16px, so that the header and its panel read as a single block. The panel of a branch header keeps the width of its column. The explanation of the tension connector unfolds full width below the pair it links, in the same panel.

Typography of the explanation. The widget's sans-serif stack, 14.5px, line-height 1.65, color rgba(255,255,255,.82), line length capped at 68ch, left-aligned, paragraphs separated by 10px and starting flush with the margin. Bold turns to full white. A long explanation that calls for steps receives internal subheadings in the style of the tags: monospace 11px, uppercase, letter-spacing 0.1em, white at 0.6. The explanation's text is selectable and its clicks belong to it: the reader selects and copies freely there, and folding is commanded from the element.

Signal of the unfolded state. The unfolded element carries a 2px left side bar in full white, set inside its visual frame via an inset box-shadow (inset 2px 0 0 #ffffff), in the language of the widget's side bars. The signal is unique and strictly identical on a card, a header or the banner, and the reader recognizes it at a glance.

For cards, the bar is added to the existing shadows of the card body and runs the full height of the enlarged card. Box-shadows replace one another, so each state redeclares the complete list: the bar of the unfolded state, the inset hairline and the fine line of light at the top. In the unfolded state the hairline keeps its resting value, 0.2 alpha, the bar being the only signal of unfolding. Typical form: box-shadow: inset 2px 0 0 #ffffff, inset 0 0 0 .5px rgba(255,255,255,.2), inset 0 1px 0 rgba(255,255,255,.04). On hover of an unfolded card, the hover rule redeclares the same list with the hairline at 0.35, the bar staying in place. The whole follows the clip-path and stays within the card's visual frame.

For elements without a frame of their own (section titles, subgroup headers, branch headers, headings, column labels, banner), the hover background band becomes permanent and extends to the explanation panel, which gives the bar a surface: rgba(20,20,20,.92) for headers, headings and labels, rgba(255,255,255,.015) for the banner, the bar being added there by box-shadow:inset 2px 0 0 #ffffff and running the full height of the header and the panel together. The banner's corners keep their normal color. The tension connector, the only thread-like element to carry an explanation, turns full white when its explanation is unfolded.

Unfold sign. The section title carries a “+” sign in monospace, which turns 45° and becomes “×” when the explanation is unfolded, in transform .3s. The section header brings together two commands, opening the section and the title's explanation: the sign designates the second. It is set as a superscript just after the title text, at 13px, according to the superscript rule. Color rgba(255,255,255,.32) at rest, rgba(255,255,255,.6) on hover of the header within the fine-pointer media query, full white in the unfolded state. The sign commands the title's explanation, and the rest of the header commands the section. All the other unfoldable elements (banner, block headings, subgroup and branch headers, column labels, cards, timeline cards, tension connector) unfold with a click on the whole element and do without a sign: their reaction on hover and the white bar of the unfolded state say by themselves that they open.

The signal always goes through an inset box-shadow, which follows the clip-path and stays exactly within the visible limits of the unfolded element, leaving the tree line and the neighboring connectors intact.

The switch to the unfolded state and back is instantaneous for the bar, which is a state signal; the explanation, for its part, unfolds according to “Unfolding an explanation” under ANIMATIONS.

TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE

Container structure. The outer container #scroll leaves its height free and its overflow visible. One and the same file reads as it is in the browser and in the host page that embeds it. The inner container #w receives padding:32px 24px 0 and position:relative, essential to positioning the global arrow. The body keeps its bottom edge free. The CSS lives in a <style> block, the JS inline. The code is written with reusable functions and without any external library: richness goes to the content, and the code stays compact.

Box model. The stylesheet opens with `*{box-sizing:border-box}`: every explicit dimension in the widget (44px touch boxes, minimum heights, box widths) then includes the padding, and all the reference measurements given here apply as they are.

Width and horizontal overflow. Every card, every header and every grid cell receives `min-width:0`, and all the widget's text `overflow-wrap:anywhere`: each cell keeps the width the grid gives it, whatever the length of a word, and the document's scroll width stays equal to that of the window. Titles and labels (banner, headings, section, subgroup, branch and card titles, column labels) receive `overflow-wrap:break-word` and `hyphens:auto`, the document carrying `lang="en"`: a long word moves to the next line whole, or breaks at a syllable with its hyphen, following English hyphenation. `html`, `body`, `#scroll` and `#w` keep their overflow visible, which preserves the anchoring of all the widget's sticky elements, section headers included.

Content and language. All the widget's texts, explanations included, are written in English, and the page carries `lang="en"`. The visible content uses commas, parentheses or colons as the context requires. Every element representing a concept, a category or a detail carries a data-name and contains its explanation, written in HTML paragraphs inside the unfolding wrapper. Unfolding is handled by delegation on #w: a click on an unfoldable element toggles its unfold class, and a click whose target lies inside an explanation panel stays with the panel.

CSS class naming. Each family of classes receives an explicit prefix of at least three letters (bcn for banner-corner, tml for timeline, ten for tension, blk for block), and modifiers attach to that prefix (bcn-tl, bcn-tr). Each name is unique throughout the widget, the family prefix carrying that uniqueness.

Superscript and line height. The unfold sign set as a superscript to the section title is taken out of the line calculation: `display:inline-block;line-height:0;position:relative;top:-.35em`. This offset by position leaves the line height unchanged. The sign keeps a 20px box, `padding:10px;margin:-10px`, the negative margin absorbing the footprint of the padding.

State of the sign. The base state, outside any media query, is visibility at rgba(255,255,255,.32). Inside the fine-pointer media query (`@media (hover:hover) and (pointer:fine)`), it rises to rgba(255,255,255,.6) on hover of its element. In the unfolded state, it turns full white in every regime. A general rule of the widget: what is seen is what is clicked, and any element made transparent receives `pointer-events:none`, which it gets back as `auto` together with its visibility.

WIDGET STRUCTURE

Banner. Main title centered, in white #ffffff, uppercase, letter-spacing 0.18em, borderless. Subtitle centered, monospace, white, letter-spacing 0.08em. On hover, the title lights up with a white text-shadow. Four decorative L-shaped corners at the four angles, each 16x16px, in position:absolute at the exact corners (top:0/left:0, top:0/right:0, bottom:0/left:0, bottom:0/right:0), flush with the frame: two perpendicular 1px borders in rgba(255,255,255,0.3) at rest, rising to rgba(255,255,255,0.6) on hover. The banner contains the title, the subtitle and the corners; the settings row, scope, depth and text size, comes next, then the diagram's first content. Any cross-cutting or complementary section adopts this same neutral white style.

Block heading. When the sections are grouped into thematic color blocks, each block opens with a heading, set above the block's first section, outside the sections. It occupies a level of its own, above level 1 of the section titles, and draws its authority from its form, its size remaining that of a label, which leaves the banner alone at the top. Form: the block's name in monospace 11px, bold, uppercase, letter-spacing 0.14em, in full white #ffffff, aligned to the container's left edge with a padding of 10px 12px, so that its first letter falls on that of the numbered tags of the sections it governs; then, on the same line and vertically centered, a 1px hairline that fills the whole remaining width, white, in a gradient from rgba(255,255,255,0.3) → 0.15 → 0.05 → transparent, the stops of a neutral section's separator: the name and the rule are both white in every widget, the block's color being read in the sections it governs. This rule gives the heading its reach: it runs above all the sections of the block and gathers them under it. The heading names, and that alone: the bullet, the tag, the frame and the chevron belong to other levels. Spacing: about 44px above, 36px below 640px, and 4px below, which attaches it to the section that follows and detaches it from the one that precedes. It stays in the flow, the section header being the permanent landmark while scrolling.

The heading of a block of several sections carries a data-name and its explanation, which sets out the overall logic of the subject the block gathers, according to the rule laid down under EXPLANATIONS. It is therefore unfoldable and receives the common treatment: dark gray band on hover with the name, white, lit by a white glow, a 2px white bar as an inset box-shadow in the unfolded state, an explanation panel below it across the full width of the container. Below 640px it receives `min-height:44px;display:flex;align-items:center` like any touch target, its padding-left remaining unchanged. Being outside the sections, it stays visible at all times, including when all the sections of its block are collapsed, which is its very function: to show the overall structure before anything is opened.

Block of a single section. When a color block gathers a single section, the heading names the block and stands as a simple landmark in the flow: it keeps its form (white name, white rule, spacing) and stays still on hover. The explanation then lives on the section, whose overview also carries the overall logic of the block: a single explanation for a single subject.

Section titles. The section header is a strict rectangle; the cut-corner clip-path belongs to the numbered tag and to the cards. Inner padding of the header: 16px 12px. Framed numbered tag (01, 02, 03…) in monospace, bold, padding 3px 8px, 1px solid border, cut corners in clip-path polygon with 4px notches, font-variant-numeric:tabular-nums to align the digits vertically from one section to the next. The number is written by a CSS counter, according to “Display” under SCOPE AND DEPTH, and so runs without gaps in every reading. The clip-path slices off the diagonal edge of the bevel, which thus remains without a stroke: that is the intended rendering. For neutral sections, tag and border in rgba(255,255,255,0.7); for colored sections, in the accent color. On hover of the header, the tag's border brightens in its own color.

Section separator. Height 1px, just below the title. A gradient with four successive stops toward transparent, more organic than a linear fade. Colored sections: accent color at 0.7 alpha → 0.35 → 0.12 → 0.04 → transparent. Neutral sections: rgba(255,255,255,0.3) → 0.15 → 0.05 → transparent. The separator has opacity:0 in its base CSS and opacity:1 under its section's open class: it underlines the title of an open section, and the animation carries it from one state to the other. Carrying the visible state by the class guarantees that it stays in place once the animations are cancelled, at the end of the opening.

Sticky headers. The section header takes position:sticky with top:0 when its section is expanded, and stays in the flow when it is collapsed. Background rgba(10,10,10,0.92) with backdrop-filter:blur(8px), always doubled with -webkit-backdrop-filter:blur(8px), for a semi-transparent veil that blurs the content scrolling behind and keeps a permanent landmark in continuity. The sticky header takes z-index:5, above the cards scrolling past; see the isolation rule of the special layouts.

Collapsible sections. Each section has an independent collapse and expand element: a `<span>` showing the chevron ›, at the right end of the header, in monospace, size 20px, padding 4px 10px, borderless, in rgba(255,255,255,.45) at rest and white on hover. The header commands the opening: a click anywhere on the header, outside the title's “+” sign, toggles its section's open class, through a data-tog handled by delegation, and the chevron carries the sign of it. Open section: rotate(90deg); collapsed section: rotate(0). Default transform-origin center. Transition transform .3s, color .25s. Each section also carries a `data-cle`, described under RETAINED STATE. On first load, all sections are collapsed; on a later visit, the sections that the retained state names come back open. Below 640px it receives a touch box that leaves the header at its height, see Touch targets.

Explanation of the section title. The section title names a subject, and its overall explanation waits folded like all the others. The “+” sign placed after the title commands it: a click on this sign unfolds or folds it, whether the section is open or collapsed, and a click elsewhere on the header opens or collapses the section. The explanation unfolds in a panel placed just below the header, outside the section's collapsible content, in the style of the explanation, text aligned on the first letter of the title, width capped at 68ch; the header and its panel receive together the dark gray band and the white bar of the unfolded state. It says what the section is about and how its elements fit together.

Global arrow →. At the top right, it toggles all the sections with one click. `<span tabindex="0" role="button">` containing the single character →. It is a direct child of #w and placed before the banner in the HTML. An invisible 40x40px box in position:absolute, display:flex to center the character. The arrow is anchored on the banner's upper-right decorative L-shaped corner: its box nestles in the hollow of the corner, below the horizontal stroke of the L and to the left of its vertical stroke, at a strictly equal distance from the two strokes. This equality gives the visual anchoring. Reference gap: 16px on each side, that is top:48px;right:40px for #w in position:relative with padding:32px 24px 0: the horizontal stroke of the L runs along the top edge of the banner, 32px from the top of #w, its vertical stroke along the right edge of the banner, 24px from the right edge of #w, and the 40x40px box set at top:48px;right:40px therefore leaves 16px toward each of the two strokes, the arrow's axis falling at an equal distance from both. The arrow, at z-index:10, captures its own clicks on its box; the unfolding and the hover of the banner stay active everywhere else on it. Monospace character, font-size:60px, line-height:1, color:rgba(255,255,255,.45) at rest, white with text-shadow:0 0 12px rgba(255,255,255,.4) on hover. Transition transform .3s ease, color .25s, text-shadow .25s.

State of the arrow. Two rotation states: rotate(90deg) when everything is collapsed, arrow pointing down, and rotate(-90deg) as soon as at least one section is open, arrow pointing up. The state is recomputed after every change, individual toggles included, which keeps it consistent with the action the next click will produce. On click, the arrow collapses everything as soon as a section is open, and expands everything otherwise, in a cascade in both cases, see ANIMATIONS.

Card borders. A 0.5px hairline border implemented via an inset box-shadow, for a lighter rendering on high-density screens. A fine inner line of light at the top, a second inset box-shadow (inset 0 1px 0 rgba(255,255,255,0.04)), which gives depth while leaving the black intact. At rest, the hairline is at 0.2 alpha and rises to 0.35 on hover, which firms up the outline at the same time as the halo appears. As with the numbered tag, the clip-path slices off the edge of the bevel, which remains without a stroke: that is the intended rendering.

SCOPE AND DEPTH

Two choices for the reader. The reader chooses two things, with one click each: the scope, which says what ground the widget shows them, and the depth, which says how deeply they go through it. Three scopes, Core, Broad, Complete, and three depths, Abridged, Standard, Full, combine freely: the same widget thus offers nine readings, from Core–Abridged, which gives the heart of the subject in a few strokes, to Complete–Full, which covers the whole ground in detail.

Three scopes. Before writing, the AI draws up the map of the subject: its heart, then the domains that surround it, as it discerns them for this subject. From it, it derives three nested perimeters, each defined by the question it answers: the core scope shows what one needs to know to be able to say one knows the subject; the broad scope adds what is needed to understand why the subject is as it is: what grounds it, what surrounds it and what follows directly from it; the complete scope adds everything that a connoisseur of the subject judges worthy of being attached to it, up to the domains whose link remains illuminating. These three questions serve as a test: the AI asks them of each section and each element to set its scope, and the connoisseur's judgment draws the outer limit of the complete scope. The widget is written on the whole map, and each scope shows the part of it that belongs to it.

Three depths. The abridged depth gives the skeleton of the subject and the essentials of each element, the standard depth unfolds the reasoning, the full depth carries the detail and the nuances. Writing proceeds in this direction, from level 1 to level 3, each level inserting itself into a text that already held together.

Cumulative attributes. Each element carries its entry scope in the `data-a` attribute and its entry depth in the `data-n` attribute: a level 1 element appears in all three choices, a level 2 element from broad or standard onward, a level 3 element in complete or full only. An element without an attribute counts as 1. The two attributes are set independently. Scope is set first on whole sections, a neighboring domain most often forming its own sections; it is also set, inside a central section, on a subgroup, a card, a tension pair, a timeline event or an explanation paragraph, where the content links the heart to a neighboring domain. Depth is set mostly on the paragraphs of the explanations, and also on the cards, subgroups, pairs and events that belong to detail. A fork keeps its two branches in every reading: the attributes are set there on the whole section or on the cards of the branches. A block heading carries the lowest levels of its sections, and appears as soon as one of them appears.

Each reading stands on its own. The core scope reads as a whole widget on the heart of the subject, and the wider scopes add their elements to it at their place in the reading order. Each explanation opens with a level 1 paragraph that says the essentials of its element, so that every rendered element keeps its explanation in the abridged depth; the standard and full depths add their paragraphs to it. What an explanation says about an element of a wider scope stands in a paragraph that carries that scope.

Display. The choices live in two sets of body classes, `a1`, `a2`, `a3` and `v1`, `v2`, `v3`, and the stylesheet removes from rendering whatever exceeds the chosen reading: `body.a1 [data-a="2"],body.a1 [data-a="3"],body.a2 [data-a="3"],body.v1 [data-n="2"],body.v1 [data-n="3"],body.v2 [data-n="3"]{display:none}`. The scope buttons carry `data-amp` and the depth buttons `data-v`, which leaves `data-a` and `data-n` to the content alone. The numbered tags are counted over the rendered sections: #w receives `counter-reset:sec`, each section `counter-increment:sec`, and the tag writes its number in a ::before through `counter(sec,decimal-leading-zero)`, the tag staying empty in the HTML. A section removed from rendering leaves the count, and the numbering runs without gaps in every reading.

Sections from one reading to another. A section keeps its open state when the reading removes it from rendering, and finds it again when it comes back; an unfolded explanation likewise keeps its state. The global arrow, its cascade and the calculation of its state apply to the rendered sections, recognized by `offsetParent!==null`. Changing the reading is instantaneous, like changing the size: at each change, the drawing functions of the open sections are called again, the state of the global arrow is recomputed, the measured height is announced to the host page and the state goes out to the keepers.

Opening reading. The widget opens at the broad scope and the full depth: the classes `a2` and `v3` are set on body in the HTML, which makes the widget appear in this reading from the first frame, and the script declares them at the top. The retained state then restores the reader's choices.

Settings row. The scope, depth and text size settings stand on a single row, a direct child of #w placed just after the banner: `display:flex;flex-wrap:wrap;align-items:center;justify-content:space-between;gap:12px 24px;margin:20px 0 0`, in this order from left to right. Each setting forms a group `display:flex;align-items:center;gap:10px`, preceded by its label in monospace 10px, uppercase, letter-spacing 0.14em, white at 0.45: SCOPE, DEPTH, TEXT SIZE. The row wraps between the groups when the width requires it; below 640px, each group takes its own line, left-aligned. Labels and buttons keep their size at every text size step.

Scope and depth buttons. Each group lines up three `<button type="button">`, carrying `data-amp` at 1, 2 and 3 for the scope, `data-v` at 1, 2 and 3 for the depth, in a family of classes with the prefix `lec`, joined into one continuous segment, `margin-left:-1px` from the second onward, in the vocabulary of the numbered tag: transparent background, hairline as an inset box-shadow 0 0 0 .5px rgba(255,255,255,.2), monospace 10px, uppercase, letter-spacing 0.1em, padding 7px 10px, white at 0.55. The last button of each segment carries the tag's cut corner, a 4px notch at the top right, which makes the segment a single piece. The active button turns full white, with a full white hairline. On hover, within the fine-pointer media query, the hairline goes to 0.35 and the text to full white. Below 640px, each button receives a height of 44px.

TEXT SIZE

Setting. The reader sets the text size with three buttons, A−, A, A+, and the widget keeps the setting from one visit to the next, along with the opening of the sections. The setting is an enlargement factor, `--dz`, set on the page's root element. All the widget's text sizes are written `calc(Npx * var(--dz,1.25))`, N being the reference size given in these instructions, and so follow the factor in a single movement.

Steps. Six steps lead from tight text to ample text: 0.85, 0.925, 1, 1.08, 1.16, 1.25. The widget opens at the most ample step, 1.25, which is its default size: this step is also the fallback value of each `var(--dz,1.25)`, so that the widget appears at this size from the first frame. The A− button goes down one step, the A+ button goes up one step, the middle A button brings the widget back to its default size. The button that reaches the end of the range fades, to 0.3 opacity with `pointer-events:none`: on opening, A+ thus stands back and tells the reader that the text is at its largest.

What follows the factor. All text that the reader reads follows the factor, from the banner title to the last card, explanations included. The controls (chevron, global arrow, unfold sign, settings row), the tension connector's glyph and the whole geometry of the widget keep their measurements: lines, dots, corners, grid indents, container paddings and touch boxes. The structure thus stays still while the text breathes.

Derived measurements. A measurement derived from a text size carries the factor in the same calculation. The padding-left of a subgroup header is written `calc(11px - Fpx * var(--dz,1.25) / 2)`, F being the reference size of its title, which keeps the first letter on the line at 32px at every step. The vertical position of the timeline dot is likewise written with the factor, and the dot falls on the middle of the first line of its card's title at every step. At each change of step, the drawing functions of the open sections are called again, as on resize, and the measured height is announced to the host page.

Place and form. The setting forms the right-hand group of the settings row, described under SCOPE AND DEPTH, preceded by its label TEXT SIZE. The three controls are `<button type="button">` elements carrying `data-tz` at -1, 0 and 1, in a family of classes with the prefix `tzr`. Each receives the vocabulary of the numbered tag: transparent background, hairline as an inset box-shadow 0 0 0 .5px rgba(255,255,255,.2), cut corner in clip-path with a 4px notch, min-width 38px, padding 7px 9px, sans-serif in white at 0.7. The three letters grow in size with their meaning, 11px for A−, 13px for A, 15px for A+, which makes the setting readable at a glance. On hover, within the fine-pointer media query, the hairline goes to 0.35 and the letter to full white with a subtle white text-shadow. Below 640px, each button receives a 44x44px box and the label stays on the line.

Script. The handler, shared by the buttons of the settings row, is set by delegation on #w. The `TZ` table holds the six steps and its last step is the opening size. A size function sets the factor on the root element through `setProperty('--dz',v)`, fades the button that reaches the end of the range, calls the drawings again and announces the height; on click, the state goes out to the keepers. It is called a first time on load with the opening size, then at each click, and by the application of the retained state.

SECTION LAYOUTS

Choice of layout. For each section, ask: what does someone unfamiliar with the subject need to know, in what order, in what form? The form follows from the function.

Two families exist. The standard layout is a grid of cards, in sequential mode (one column) for ordered elements (narrative, chronology, steps), in parallel mode (adaptive multi-column) for independent elements (typology, themes, classification). The special layouts are the fork, the tension and the vertical timeline, described below. Other structures will be added as the user updates the instructions; in the meantime, composition is done with those that are described.

STANDARD LAYOUT

Three distinct visual levels.

Level 1, section titles: framed numbered tag, uppercase title, gradient separator.

Level 2, subgroup headers. A solid 7px disc precedes the title, in the accent color or in white dimmed to 70% for neutral sections, followed by the name then the description in monospace, gap 14px between the three elements in normal flow, the disc staying attached to the title in the flow. The subgroup aligns to the left edge of the section container (margin-left:0). Inner padding of the header: 10px at top and bottom, 12px on the right, and on the left the value calculated below. At rest, the bullet alone signals; on hover, the dark gray band applies normally. The spacing between successive subgroups comes from a margin-top:18px carried by each subgroup from the second onward. The subgroup and its grid live directly in the section content, which keeps the grid's line aligned with that of sections without subgroups.

The subgroup's padding-left is calculated so that the visual middle of the title's first capital letter falls precisely on the tree line 32px from the edge of the container. With a 7px disc and a 14px gap, the left edge of the letter is at padding-left + 21, and its center about half the font size further on: the target padding-left is therefore 32 − 21 − (font-size / 2). The font size is the rendered one, size factor included, according to “Derived measurements” under TEXT SIZE: at the opening step, 1.25, a 14px reference title gives padding-left ≈ 2.25px, a 13px title ≈ 2.9px, a 12px title 3.5px. The calculation is set directly on the subgroup's clickable header.

Level 3, cards. Each card has three textual levels: a title (full white, bold, letter-spacing 0.04em) followed immediately by a description (white rgba at 0.55 at rest, line-height 1.5), which are read at rest, then the explanation, unfolded on click according to “Unfolding explanations”. A reference or a keyword that accompanies the title is integrated into the description as prose. The card body carries: inner padding 14px 18px, background #000000, a neutral hairline border via an inset box-shadow, a fine inner line of light at the top, a single cut corner at the top right: clip-path:polygon(0 0,calc(100% - 12px) 0,100% 12px,100% 100%,0 100%); the wrapper carries the halo, according to the halo rule. The card grid is offset to margin-left:56px from the edge of the container, which keeps the tree line rigorously aligned from one section to the next. Each card receives `min-width:0` and its text `overflow-wrap:anywhere`. The hover halo goes through filter:drop-shadow, according to the halo rule.

Tree line. A fine, continuous vertical line, 1px in rgba(255,255,255,.12), 32px from the edge of the container, to the left of the card grid, along its whole height. It is set directly on each grid via a ::before with left:-24px relative and top and bottom margins of 4px, this value of -24px being the difference between the grid's margin (56px) and the line's position (32px), so that it follows any change to this margin and becomes -12px when the grid moves closer, to 44px, below 640px. A natural consequence: in a section with subgroups, the line is segmented between the headers; in a section without subgroups, it extends continuously over the grid alone. It is present in every section in the standard layout, in sequential as in parallel mode, and marks the subordination of the cards to their title.

Sequential and parallel modes. Sequential by default: grid-template-columns:1fr, align-items:start. Parallel on explicit activation: grid-template-columns:repeat(auto-fit,minmax(min(260px,100%),1fr)), align-items:stretch, each card's body taking height:100%. Gap:12px in both modes. The line is kept in both modes. The number of cards per subgroup or per section follows what the subject calls for.

Equal height in parallel mode. The cards of a parallel grid form a regular set: all the grid's folded cards receive the same height, that of the tallest among them, which aligns the rows with one another. The script switches the grid to align-items:start for the duration of the measurement, reads the natural height of the body of each folded card, minus the current height of its explanation wrapper, sets the largest as min-height on each of these bodies, then returns the grid to align-items:stretch. When the grid fits in a single column, each card keeps its natural height.

Unfolded card in parallel mode. An unfolded card takes the full width of the grid, grid-column:1/-1, and opens on its own row; the order of the cards remains that of the document. The cards that precede it on its row share the full width of that row: the script counts the grid's columns, identifies these cards and gives each one grid-column:span n, the columns being divided equally and the remainder going to the first ones. On folding, the card keeps the full width while its explanation folds, through a folding class set for the duration of the transition, then returns to its one-column place, and the row layout is redone at that moment.

Full last row in parallel mode. A parallel grid always forms a full block, edge to edge. When its last row holds fewer cards than the grid has columns, these cards share the full width of the row: the script counts the grid's columns, identifies the cards of the last row and gives each one grid-column:span n, the columns being divided equally and the remainder going to the first ones. This row layout belongs to the row layout function described above and is redone with it.

Animated rearrangement. Every change in the arrangement of a parallel grid, when a card unfolds as at the end of its folding, happens in continuous motion, through the technique known as FLIP: the script records the position of each card in the grid and the size of its body, applies the change, records the new values, then animates each card from its old place to the new one, in transform:translate on the wrapper, and each body from its old width and height to the new ones, overflow:hidden for the duration of the movement, over .34s in cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.3,1), through the Web Animations interface. The height of the card being unfolded remains driven by its explanation. A new change occurring during the movement cancels the running animations after recording the visible positions, which chains the movements without a jump. Under reduced motion, the change is instantaneous. The row layout and the height measurement form a single function, called at each unfolding and folding of a card, at each change of reading and of step, at each change of width and at the application of the retained state.

SPECIAL LAYOUTS

For these sections, their own visual structure takes precedence, and the tree line at 32px as well as the 56px offset belong to the standard layout. The widget's general principles remain: colors, hovers, hairline borders, cut corners, animations, transparency of structural elements. The structural elements (lines, dots, arrows, connectors) are in dimmed white. All structural figures are drawn in pure CSS; for the layouts where the alignment must fall on the actual center of the cards, the drawing is done in JavaScript after render, as specified for each. Each unfoldable element carries its data-name and its explanation as everywhere else, and the carrier of the explanation is the card or the header itself, which keeps the band and the bar on the content and leaves the axis, the dots and the lines entirely structural. The tension connector also carries an explanation, the relation it depicts being information in its own right, as described under “Tension”.

Stacking isolation. Each special layout zone (fork, tension, timeline container) receives isolation:isolate, which confines its internal z-indexes to their own context and guarantees that the section's sticky header, at z-index:5, always passes above during scrolling.

General rule of connector continuity, for structures drawn in JavaScript. All connectors are calculated from the actual positions and edges of the elements, measured via getBoundingClientRect, which makes them follow the gaps defined by margin, gap or padding. The joins between segments touch thanks to a systematic sub-pixel adjustment: for a vertical line 1px thick, left = center − 0.5; for a horizontal line 1px thick, top = y − 0.5; for a thickness thick greater than 1px, the offset is thick/2. At the joins, a slight overlap of 0.5 to 1px is added to the length of the segment and absorbs anti-aliasing. The drawing functions are called on DOMContentLoaded, at each opening of a section, at the actual end of the opening, known through the completion promise of the animations and of the height transition, and at each resize event of the window. Each drawing function runs when its section carries the open class, this test on the class being the one that stays correct while the content of a collapsed section remains in the document at zero height. The drawn segments appear with a fade, opacity 0 → 1 in .25s, which inscribes them in the overall movement.

When to use what? The form follows the function.

Fork. Two parallel columns, gap:48px, a Y fork at the top making visible the common point of origin and the separation. The fork has two branches by nature, because that is its very function: a binary choice or distinction. A division into three or more branches belongs to the standard layout in parallel mode. The grid of the two columns receives a padding-top of at least 48px, which makes the branch headers start below the legs of the Y, trunk 16px plus legs 20px plus a little air. The fork container, in position:relative and isolation:isolate, without horizontal padding, wraps the grid of the two columns, the empty div of the junction and the common cards: it serves as the reference for all the absolute elements of the figure, and since its width is that of the grid, the percentages below apply to it. Four CSS elements, in position:absolute, all in white at 40% (rgba(255,255,255,.4)), 1px wide or high, make up the Y. Vertical trunk from the top down to the point of separation: left:calc(50% - .5px), top:0, height:16px, centered on the axis according to the sub-pixel rule. Horizontal bar linking the two column centers: top:16px, left:calc(25% - 12px), right:calc(25% - 12px). Left descending leg: top:16px, height:20px, left:calc(25% - 12px). Right descending leg: top:16px, height:20px, left:calc(75% + 12px - 1px), positioned by left like the left one, which makes the 1px line fall exactly on the column center and keeps the symmetry. A fifth element, the continuation line, extends the trunk downward at left:calc(50% - .5px) from top:16px; since its length depends on the actual heights of the columns, it is drawn in JavaScript after render, like the lower junction, in the same white at 40%.

The values calc(25% - 12px) and calc(75% + 12px) correspond exactly to the column centers for gap:48px on a 1fr 1fr grid. The continuation line has strictly the same opacity as the fork and starts exactly at top:16px, at the Y's point of separation. Each branch has a clickable header, a title and an optional description in monospace, hover as a dark gray band. The header's content is centered in the column (align-items:center, text-align:center) and so falls below the descending leg. Branch title in the accent color, white on neutral sections, with a white glow on hover in both cases. The fork container lets the widget's background show through.

Lower junction. When the two branches lead to one or more common cards, the fork closes with a junction that reproduces exactly the geometry of the fork, upside down: legs rising from each column, a horizontal bar linking them, a trunk descending toward the common cards. When the branches end without common cards, the continuation line descends to the bottom of the grid and stops there. Since the two columns almost always have different heights, the junction is drawn in JavaScript after render, on actual measurements. Each leg starts 12px below the last card of its column, at left:calc(25% - 12px) and left:calc(75% + 12px - 1px) like the legs of the Y, and descends to the bar. The bar is set 32px below the lower of the two last cards, at left:calc(25% - 12px) and right:calc(25% - 12px). The continuation line, which already descends in the center from the Y's point of separation, crosses the bar and extends 16px beyond it: it acts as the trunk, and ends exactly at the top edge of the first common card. An empty div placed after the grid holds the necessary height, equal to the bar's position plus 16px minus the bottom of the grid. Sub-pixel: bar at top:y - 0.5, legs extended by 0.5px at the join. The drawing is called again at the opening of the section, at the actual end of the opening and on resize, like every structure drawn in JavaScript.

Tension. Two terms of the same rank that pull on each other, repeated in pairs. This layout is reserved for this precise case. The reason for the layout is that a standard grid in parallel mode arranges the cards from left to right row by row, which breaks up the couples: the tension preserves the pairing and the reading order.

Each pair is an autonomous grid, grid-template-columns:1fr 1fr, gap:96px, align-items:stretch, margin-top:12px. The two cards of a pair thus have the same height by construction, the body of each card taking height:100%, which makes the connector fall at mid-height of both by construction. An isolated term, or a concluding card, goes full width and reads alone: the structure pairs what is paired.

The connector is a character. It occupies the gap between the columns, in position:absolute, top:0, bottom:0, left:calc(50% - 48px), width:96px, display:flex centered, z-index:1, in monospace 32px, line-height:1, rgba(255,255,255,.45) at rest, white with text-shadow:0 0 12px rgba(255,255,255,.45) on hover.

Four glyphs make up the vocabulary. ↔ (U+2194), reciprocal: each holds the other, the two are of the same rank, removing one brings down the other. This is the most frequent case and the one to return to when in doubt. → (U+2192), oriented: the first term produces the second and the direction holds. ↻ (U+21BB), cyclical: each sustains the other in a loop, where a simple arrow would suggest an end point. ≡ (U+2261), false opposition: two terms that a tradition has set against each other and that say the same thing from two angles. The choice of glyph is itself information about the subject. A neighboring case has its own form: two incompatible terms between which one must choose call for a fork.

Availability of the glyphs. These four characters belong to the basic Unicode blocks, covered by all system fonts, on phone as on computer: the connector keeps the widget's monospace stack and renders identically everywhere.

The connector carries its data-name and its explanation: it sets out the relation, what leads from one term to the other, and belongs properly to this element. Being thread-like, it turns full white when its explanation is unfolded, and the explanation opens full width below the pair. The columns are named only once, at the head of the section, by two monospace labels 11px, letter-spacing 0.05em, white at 0.55, centered in their column, padding 10px 12px, unfoldable with a dark gray band on hover and an explanation of their own. They are brief and nominal (“love”, “its distortion”). This single naming allows the cards of the right-hand column each to carry a title of their own that is informative. The container receives isolation:isolate. In the animation cascade, each pair counts as one element, connector included.

Vertical timeline. A fine vertical axis, 1px in rgba(255,255,255,.15), on the left, punctuated by circular dots at the events. Each dot is 11px in diameter, in white at 70% visual opacity, surrounded by a ring of #0a0a0a background (box-shadow:0 0 0 4px #0a0a0a) that detaches it from the axis. Always white. The center of the dot coincides exactly with the axis: for an axis at 32px and an 11px dot, left edge of the dot at 26.5px (32 − 5.5). Vertically, the center of the dot aligns with the middle of the first line of its card's title, which anchors each dot to the event it represents. The dot is opaque over the axis: a translucent white layer is superimposed on an opaque black background via background:linear-gradient(rgba(255,255,255,.7),rgba(255,255,255,.7)),#0a0a0a, a rendering identical to white at 70% with the axis hidden behind the dot. Each timeline element receives padding-left:56px relative to the axis placed at 32px, that is 24px of air between the dot and the card's title. The carrier of the explanation is the event's card; the wrapper, the axis and the dots are structural. Hover and the unfolded state apply to the card alone, which leaves the axis continuous between the events. The timeline container lets the widget's background show through.

ANIMATIONS

Principle. An opening reads as an unfolding, a closing as a packing up, and the page keeps its continuity: the height follows the fade, and every element enters and leaves with a fade. Entrances are slower than exits, and every cascade, whatever the density of the section, finishes within a few tenths of a second.

Animated height. Collapsing goes through three nested levels, and these three levels together give a height that animates.

The first, the wrapper, is a single-row grid (`display:grid`) whose height goes from `grid-template-rows:0fr`, section collapsed, to `grid-template-rows:1fr`, section open, with `transition:grid-template-rows .4s cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.3,1)` on opening and `.3s cubic-bezier(.4,0,1,1)` on collapsing. This technique needs no measurement in JavaScript and works in all current browsers. The wrapper leaves its overflow free: overflow is handled one level down.

Two distinct classes carry the state of the section. The expansion class sets the `1fr` and the opening transition; it is added at the very first instant of the opening and removed at the very first instant of the collapse, which makes the height start moving in both directions at the moment of the click. The collapsing transition is declared in the wrapper's base CSS and the opening one under the expansion class, the browser taking the transition of the arrival state. The open class carries the visible separator, the sticky header and the marker for the drawing functions; it is added at the same first instant of the opening and removed at the actual end of the collapse, which keeps the section readable and measurable while it closes.

The second, the collapser, is the wrapper's only child. It carries exactly two properties: `min-height:0`, which allows the row to shrink below the height of its content, and `overflow:hidden`, which lets it actually be shorter than what it contains. It carries that alone, so its height can fall to zero: padding, borders and margins live at the next level, whose height is clipped along with the rest.

The third, the content, is the collapser's child and carries the section's inner padding, `padding:12px 0 28px`, without horizontal padding: the edge of the content is the edge of the section container, from which the tree line at 32px and the grid indents are measured.

Visibility and the release of overflow are set on the collapser. Collapsed section at rest: `visibility:hidden`, which puts the content out of reach of the eye and of the click. During the transition, in both directions: `visibility:visible` and `overflow:hidden`. Open section at rest: the `overflow:hidden` is lifted, which lets the hover halo of the cards on the right edge spread over its 16px. The JS sets these values as inline style on the collapser: `visibility:visible` and `overflow:hidden` at the first instant of any movement, in both directions; the `overflow:hidden` lifted at the actual end of the opening, known through the transitionend event on the wrapper or the equivalent promise; `visibility:hidden` at the actual end of the collapse. These three levels leave the sticky behavior intact, the section header being a sibling of the wrapper. Since the content remains in the document, the drawing functions take their measurements on the sections that carry the open class.

Sequential appearance. When a section opens, the content appears progressively, at the same time as the height unfolds. Entrance animation: opacity 0 → 1 and translateY(8px) → 0, over 0.45s in cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.7, 0.3, 1). Cascade: separator at 0ms, inner content from 80ms. For the inner content, cumulative delays in DOM order: subgroups in cascade, 80ms between each, their cards in an inner cascade, 40ms between each; for the special layouts, structural elements at 40ms intervals, in DOM order, lines and strokes first, cards and headers next, which composes the structure before the content is inscribed in it.

Header outside the cascade. The section header stays still, on opening as on collapsing. It is the only element visible in both states, open section as collapsed section, and its stability is what the user sees under their finger at the moment of the click. Its own movement comes from elsewhere and is enough: the chevron pivots in 0.3s, the separator appears below it, and it becomes sticky. The cascade therefore begins at the separator.

Cascade ceiling. The intervals above are nominal values, right for a section of a dozen elements. The last element of a section starts its animation at the latest 600ms after the click: when the sum of the nominal intervals exceeds this value, all the section's intervals are reduced in the same proportion, with a floor of 12ms that preserves the staggering. The calculation is made on the actual list of the section's animated elements, once, at the moment of opening. Closing has its own ceiling, given further on.

Triggering and animation interface. The animation is driven by JavaScript, the height starting from zero and the delays depending on the actual content. It goes through the browser's Web Animations interface, `element.animate(keyframes, options)`. Each call returns an animation object that starts over from the beginning at each opening, cancels cleanly through `cancel()` and signals its actual end through `finished`, all without layout recalculation, which keeps the movement smooth on a phone up to several hundred cards. A function animateSection(sec) is called at each opening, section toggle or global arrow. It goes through the separator then the animated elements of the content, calls `animate()` on each with its keyframes, `duration:450`, `easing:'cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.3,1)'`, a `delay` according to the capped cascade and `fill:'both'`, and keeps the returned objects in a list stored on the section. The fill both holds the initial state of the animation, opacity 0 and translateY, during the waiting delay, so that each element remains invisible until its turn in the cascade and enters in one go. The same rule applies on closing. The end of the opening is known exactly through the joint promise of all the animations in the list and of the height transition: that is the moment when the overflow is released, the JavaScript structures are drawn and the cleanup takes place.

Closing. An exit is perceived as faster than an entrance, and the user, who has clicked on the header, sees the section close before their eyes starting from the top. Closing therefore receives its own animation, opacity 1 → 0 and translateY(0) → translateY(-6px), over 0.25s in cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 1, 1), a curve that accelerates toward the end of its course, the reverse of the opening, in a reverse cascade from the last element toward the separator, with nominal intervals of 15ms between elements and 30ms between subgroups, and a ceiling of 150ms on the last start. The height transition toward 0fr, in .3s, starts with the removal of the expansion class, at the same instant as the first exit animation, so that the section packs up in about three tenths of a second, whatever its size. The open class is removed at the actual end of the whole, known through the joint promise of the animations and of the transition, then the cleanup applies.

The reader's place on closing. When a section collapses while its header is fixed at the top of the screen, the reader stands in the middle of its content. At the first instant of the collapse, the page therefore settles on the top of the section, through an immediate `scrollTo` that brings the top edge of the section to the top edge of the window, then the closing plays. The header thus stays at the very place where the reader touched it, the content packs up below it, and the next section comes to settle just below: the reader keeps their bearings. The settling applies to the collapse of a single section as to the global cascade, and concerns the section whose header is fixed, recognized by the top of the section lying above the edge of the window.

Anti-collision lock. Each section carries a lock: the list of its running animation objects, the identifier of its possible global cascade timeout, and the direction of its movement. When the user toggles a section while an animation is running on it, for example by reopening during the closing, the lock first cancels everything running on this section, `cancel()` on each animation in the list, cancellation of the timeout, removal of the transition-end listener, then starts the new movement, which leaves the section in a clean visual state. The height transition takes care of itself: changing the target value midway makes it start again from its current position, exactly the desired behavior. The lock also covers the timeouts of the global cascade: each timeout scheduled by the arrow to open or close a section is recorded both in a global registry and in the lock of its section. The global registry is emptied at each click on the arrow, which lets a total collapse triggered in the middle of an expansion take over all the sections and keeps the arrow consistent.

Visibility of the separator. The separator has opacity:0 in its base CSS and opacity:1 under the section's open class. At rest on a collapsed section, its opacity is 0; the entrance animation brings it to 1 in the opening cascade; on closing, the exit animation brings it back to 0 before the class is removed. It is the class, and not the animation, that carries the visible state of an open section at rest, which keeps it in place after the cleanup. The separator thus accompanies exactly the open state of its section.

Cleanup after opening and after closing. At the actual end of an opening, all the animations in the section's list are cancelled through `cancel()`, which returns each element to its base style, identical to the final state of the keyframes: header visible, separator visible through the open class, content visible. This cleanup frees the compositing layers retained by the animations, sticky header with backdrop-filter included, and keeps scrolling light on a widget of several hundred cards. At the actual end of a closing, the open class is removed, the content receives `visibility:hidden`, then all the animations are cancelled in the same way, which returns the header and the rest to their base state. After the cleanup, the base state takes over again, exactly as designed: header visible, separator invisible through its base opacity:0, content at zero height and invisible.

Global cascade on clicking the arrow. On total expansion, the closed sections open one after another, in document order; sections already open keep their state and let their turn pass. The interval between two starts is constant, 120ms, and the whole is capped, the last start falling at the latest 1000ms after the click; beyond that, the interval is reduced accordingly. Each section then plays its own internal animation, capped as described above, and the openings overlap: a wave travels down the page in a little over a second. On total collapse, the open sections close from the last toward the first, with a constant interval of 60ms between starts and a ceiling of 500ms on the last start, each playing its complete closing, for the effect of a packing up from bottom to top that finishes in under a second. When only one section is concerned, it opens or closes immediately. The intervals are calculated on the filtered list of the sections concerned. The separators take part in the cascade; the titles stay still. After any cascade, the rotation state of the global arrow is recomputed according to the arrow state rule.

Reduced motion. Under `@media (prefers-reduced-motion:reduce)`, a common setting on phones, the entrance and exit animations go to a duration of 0.01ms, the wrapper's height transition too, and all cascade delays to 0, those of the global cascade included. The JS reads this setting through `matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion:reduce)')` and passes these values to the `animate()` calls, the durations being driven from the JS. Sections open and close instantly, the separator stays visible on an open section through the open class, the cleanup and the anti-collision lock apply unchanged, and the structures drawn in JavaScript are drawn immediately.

Unfolding an explanation. The explanation reuses the animated height of the sections, more briefly: a single-row grid wrapper goes from `grid-template-rows:0fr` to `1fr`, in .35s cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.3,1) on unfolding and in .25s cubic-bezier(.4,0,1,1) on folding; its only child, the collapser, carries `min-height:0` and `overflow:hidden`; the text, at the next level, fades in, opacity 0 → 1 and translateY(6px) → 0 over .35s. On folding, the text fades out, opacity 1 → 0 over .2s in cubic-bezier(.4,0,1,1), while the height folds: the exit answers the entrance, and the explanation fades away instead of being cut off by the edge. The exit animation keeps its final state through `fill:'forwards'` for the duration of the folding and is cancelled at the next unfolding, just before the entrance animation. The inner separator of the cards follows the same fade. Under reduced motion, unfolding is instantaneous. When a section collapses, the unfolded explanations of its elements keep their state and are found unfolded again on reopening.

RETAINED STATE

Principle. The widget gives the reader back the arrangement in which they left it: the scope, depth and text size they had chosen come back, the sections they had opened come back open, the global arrow matches this arrangement, and the reading position falls on the page thus constituted. The state covers the scope, the depth, the text size and the opening of the sections, which persist from one visit to the next; unfolded explanations belong to the present moment and are unfolded again at each visit.

Two keepers. The widget entrusts its state to two keepers and serves both of them at each change. The browser's local storage keeps it when the widget is opened directly, as a file or as a page. The host page keeps it when the widget is placed in an isolated frame: there, local storage belongs to the page, and the frame addresses it by message. Both writes are made inside a `try` and the widget carries on with whatever it obtains.

Section key. Each section carries a `data-cle` attribute, a short word drawn from its subject, written once in the HTML: `covenant`, `prayer`, `kingship`. The state names the sections by this key, so that adding a section, rewriting or reordering lets each section find its opening again. The keys that the state and the document have in common set their sections, and the remaining sections keep their initial state, collapsed.

Form of the state. A plain object, read and written as JSON on both sides: `{v:1, ampleur:2, epaisseur:3, taille:1.25, ouvertes:["covenant","prayer"]}`, where `v` carries the form number, `ampleur` the chosen scope, `epaisseur` the chosen depth, `taille` the retained step and `ouvertes` the list of the keys of the open sections. A state that carries a step absent from the table returns the widget to its opening size, and a scope or depth outside 1, 2, 3 to the opening reading. The local storage key is `'diag-etat-'` followed by a widget identifier, declared once at the top of the script and drawn from the banner title.

Sending. The state is recomposed from the document, through the list of the sections carrying the expansion class, which states from the click onward the direction the reader wants, and goes out to both keepers at once, in the very gesture: at each section toggle, on clicking the global arrow as at each change of scope, depth or step. It also goes out when the page is hidden or left, on `visibilitychange` to the hidden state and on `pagehide`. Toward the host page, the sending is written `parent.postMessage({lvEtat:etat},'*')`, and it takes place when the widget is actually placed in a frame, which the comparison `parent!==window` establishes: opened alone in a tab, the widget is its own host page, and local storage is enough for it.

Request. At the first instant of the script, the widget reads local storage and asks the host page for its state through `parent.postMessage({lvEtatVoulu:1},'*')`, under the same condition `parent!==window`. The page answers with a message `{lvEtat:etat}`, and it may repeat it several times during setup. The widget accepts the messages whose source is its parent and applies the received state at each arrival, until the reader's first gesture: the page's state, keeper of the framed regime, then becomes that of the widget. From the first gesture onward, the reader leads, and the widget keeps its current state.

Application. The application first sets the retained scope, depth and step, then the state of each section at rest, as described in ANIMATIONS. A section that the state names receives the open state: the expansion class and the open class on the section, `visibility:visible` and the overflow released on the collapser, the chevron at `rotate(90deg)`, the separator visible through the class. A section that the state leaves aside receives the collapsed state: both classes removed, `visibility:hidden` on the collapser, the chevron at `rotate(0)`. It finishes by recomputing the state of the global arrow, calling the drawing functions of the open sections and announcing the measured height. The cascade belongs to the click: a section rendered open appears open straight away, and the reader finds their page already constituted.

Resumption while the content is set up. Fonts, images and drawn structures settle after the first frames: the application is therefore replayed at 150 ms, 500 ms and 1200 ms, and each pass leaves exactly the same result, the application setting the same state on the same sections each time. The measured height is announced at each pass, which lets the host page set the reading position again on the rendered arrangement. The reader's first gesture on the widget, chevron, global arrow or settings button, ends the resumption and makes their gesture the current state.

MOBILE ADAPTATION

Principle. The widget is a standalone file, opened on a wide screen as well as in a phone's browser, as it is. A single stylesheet serves both cases, with a single breakpoint at 640px, plus a few unconditional touch rules described below. Below 640px, the visual language remains whole, #0a0a0a background, cut corners, hairlines, monospace tags, white bar of the unfolded state: only the geometries that need width are reworked. The content is the same on both sides: all the sections, all the cards and all the explanations are present identically.

Breakpoint boundary. The mobile CSS is written `@media (max-width:639.98px)` and the JavaScript tests `matchMedia('(min-width:640px)')`. The two are then strictly complementary, and each width belongs to a single regime, including at exactly 640px and at the fractional widths that zoom produces. The same complementarity applies to any other width-dependent structure.

Document head. The file always contains `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width,initial-scale=1">`, which makes the page render at the actual width of the device. Manual zoom remains available. `html` also receives `-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%` and `text-size-adjust:100%`, which keeps text sizes stable on landscape rotation in Safari and preserves the pixel-calculated alignments, the padding-left of subgroup headers and the center of the timeline dots.

Unconditional touch rules. Three properties apply to every clickable element of the widget, whatever the width: `-webkit-tap-highlight-color:transparent`, which leaves the widget in control of its visual feedback, `-webkit-touch-callout:none`, which keeps a long press neutral, and `touch-action:manipulation`, which makes the tap immediate. `user-select:none` applies to the controls alone: chevron, global arrow, unfold sign, settings buttons. Cards, headers, the banner and the explanations keep their text selectable, which lets the reader copy a reference, a verse or a passage.

Hover and touch. All of the widget's hover rules live in `@media (hover:hover) and (pointer:fine)`, which reserves the halo for devices that know how to remove it and keeps the white bar as the only signal of the unfolded state on touch screens. The touch regime is its exact complement, `@media not ((hover:hover) and (pointer:fine))`, which covers screens without hover as well as devices that declare hover with a coarse pointer, Android styluses and touch hybrids; every touch-specific rule is written under this single condition. That is where the immediate feedback on tap lives, a brief `:active` state: background rgba(255,255,255,.04) on headers, cards and settings buttons, without transition. Safari on iOS applies `:active` as soon as the page has registered a touch event listener: the JS therefore sets one, even empty, on the document on load, which makes the feedback visible on iPhone. The lasting feedback remains the bar of the unfolded state.

Unfold sign on touch screens. Visibility is the sign's base state, so that on a screen without hover it is seen on every section title. Its touch target is described under “Touch targets”.

Touch targets. Every clickable element measures at least 44x44px on touch screens. The section title's sign, which alone carries the target of its explanation, receives a 44x44px box centered on the glyph, its negative margins leaving the header at its height. The correction goes through a transparent box declared with explicit dimensions, the visual size of the element remaining unchanged. The chevron › keeps its 20px size and receives `padding:0;min-width:44px;height:44px;display:flex;align-items:center;justify-content:center` below 640px, plus `margin-top:-12px;margin-bottom:-12px`, which lets its box overflow into the header's existing vertical padding: the header remains around 54px, a height that keeps the title compact once fixed as sticky. The global arrow keeps its 40x40px box on a large screen and goes to 44x44px below 640px, like each of the three size buttons; the scope and depth buttons take a height of 44px there. Subgroup headers, block headings and tension column labels receive `min-height:44px;display:flex;align-items:center` below 640px, their padding-left remaining intact, on which the calculated alignment of the subgroup title on the line at 32px depends. The headers of fork branches, being fuller, already reach the target. All these heights assume the border-box model set at the top of the stylesheet.

Banner and global arrow below 640px. The padding of #w goes to `20px 16px 0`. The decorative L-shaped corners go to 12x12px. The main title goes down in size and its letter-spacing to 0.12em, with the compensating text-indent adjusted to the same value, `text-indent:0.12em`, according to the optical recentering rule. It receives `overflow-wrap:break-word` and a side padding of 64px, which keeps a long title entirely to the left of the global arrow. The value of 64px is calculated: the banner being set back 16px from the edge of #w and the arrow's box occupying from 28px to 72px from that same edge, 64px leaves 8px of air between the title and the arrow. The arrow keeps its anchoring on the upper-right L-shaped corner and its strictly equal gap to the two strokes, with a reference gap reduced to 12px: for #w in `padding:20px 16px 0`, a 44x44px box is therefore set at `top:32px;right:28px`. The glyph goes down to 40px. The equality of the two gaps holds in the rendering as on a large screen.

Offsets below 640px. The tree line remains 32px from the edge of the container, a position on which the padding-left of subgroup headers depends, calculated by 32 − 21 − font-size/2. It is the card grids that move closer: `margin-left:44px` against 56px on a large screen, which leaves 12px of air between the line and the edge of the card against 24px. The `::before` that carries the line is set on the grid in relative coordinates: its `left` therefore goes from -24px to -12px at the same time as the margin, which keeps the line at 32px. The inner padding of the cards goes to `14px 14px`. The axis and the dots keep their position. On a 360px screen, these values give 284px of card.

Parallel mode. The parallel mode grid is written `repeat(auto-fit,minmax(min(260px,100%),1fr))`. The `min()` makes the track follow the available width as soon as it drops below 260px, which keeps the grid inside its container. This form is the correct one at every width.

EXPLANATIONS

Role. The explanations are the heart of the widget. The diagram carries within itself all the knowledge of the subject: the overall map is seen at a glance, and each element delivers on click what there is to know about it. The reader explores by unfolding what they want to go deeper into, and the widget is self-sufficient.

What an explanation conveys. The first objective is the understanding of the subject: bringing out the logic, the stakes, the scope of the element. Understanding presupposes knowing: the explanation therefore conveys in a single stretch the material and its meaning, in the form the nature of the element calls for. A narrative is told, a concept is set out, a doctrine is unfolded, a character is presented, a technique is described, an event is situated. The explanation first gives what the beginner needs to learn, what the thing is, its definition, its main distinction, then leads to what makes its meaning understood. Its length follows what the element calls for: a few sentences for a detail, several paragraphs for a notion that carries a great deal.

Overall logic. Every title in the widget, at whatever level it stands (banner, block heading, section title, subgroup header, branch header, tension column label), names a subject that gathers several elements. Its explanation takes this subject as a whole with its own meaning and sets out its overall logic: the subject understood in a single stretch, in its nature, its reason for being, its movement from beginning to end, what it reveals and what it changes. The elements placed under the title take their place within this movement, and their own explanations go deeper into them. The card, which names a single element, receives the same requirement in its measure: its explanation brings out the proper logic of what it names.

Explanation of a relation. The tension connector depicts the passage from one term to the other, and its explanation sets out this passage: it is the element of the widget whose explanation concerns a relation, the relation being what it represents.

Autonomous reading. The reader opens the explanations in the order they choose. Each one therefore names its subject in full and is understood on its own, while agreeing with the title that governs it. Technical terms receive their definition there at the place where they appear, according to the audience rule laid down at the top.

WRITING WITH SEVERAL HANDS

The widget's content can be entrusted to subagents who write in parallel, each one a part of the diagram. The main AI then keeps responsibility for the unity of the diagram, from the banner to the last element.

A common brief precedes the writing. The main AI writes it in a file that each subagent reads in full before writing. In it, it sets out the overall plan of the diagram with the scope of each element, the distribution of the material across the three depths, each one's share, the audience, the tone, the rules of substance and form, and the form in which each share is delivered.

A single-handed rereading follows the writing. Before assembly, the main AI itself reads the entire content, in the order in which the reader discovers it, from the banner to the last element. It gives all the texts one and the same voice. It grants each example and each quotation a main place, the one where they serve best, and makes them reappear elsewhere when that new place draws from them an illumination of its own that fully justifies their return. It rewrites the explanations that repeat what another already carries, replacing them with new material. The diagram then reads as the work of a single author, and each explanation keeps its autonomous reading and its full substance.
